# U33: coprimality structure of the auxiliary cubic factor R

Status: verified partial result only. This does not resolve Beal's conjecture.

Date: 2026-09-14.

## Setting

Continue from U24-U25 in the valid weighted branch

    X^10 + 256 Y^10 = Z^17.

The surviving Case III data are

    a=A^5,
    b=16*17^4*B^5,
    gcd(a,b)=1,

and, with K=Q(sqrt(17)), eta=4+sqrt(17), x=gamma, y=eta*delta,

    x^5-y^5 = (8 b^2/17) R(a,b),

where

    R(a,b)
      =(102+17 sqrt(17))a^6
       +(34+51 sqrt(17))a^4 b^2
       +(-102-85 sqrt(17))a^2 b^4
       +(-34+9 sqrt(17))b^6.

U25 proved that (x,y)=1 as ideals of O_K, so

    gcd((x-y), Phi_5(x,y))

is supported only above 5, where

    Phi_5(x,y)=x^4+x^3 y+x^2 y^2+x y^3+y^4.

This checkpoint sharpens the arithmetic support of R relative to the rational factors occurring in the U20 coefficient identity.

## 1. Dehomogenized cubic

Put t=b^2/a^2 and write

    R(a,b)=a^6 r(t),

with

    r(t)=(102+17 sqrt(17))
         +(34+51 sqrt(17))t
         +(-102-85 sqrt(17))t^2
         +(-34+9 sqrt(17))t^3.

Exact evaluation gives

    r(0)  = 17(6+sqrt(17)),
    r(1)  = -8 sqrt(17),
    r(3)  = -32(51+11 sqrt(17)),
    r(-1) = -128 sqrt(17).

The corresponding rational norms are

    Norm(r(0))  = 17^2 * 19,
    Norm(r(1))  = -2^6 * 17,
    Norm(r(3))  = -2^15 * 17,
    Norm(r(-1)) = -2^14 * 17.

These were computed exactly in Wolfram Language.

## 2. Gcd consequences with the rational factors

Because gcd(a,b)=1, any prime ideal dividing both R(a,b) and one of the rational factors below must divide the corresponding specialized value of r(t).

Therefore:

- if a prime ideal divides both R and b, its underlying rational prime is in {17,19};
- if it divides both R and a-b or a+b, its underlying rational prime is in {2,17};
- if it divides both R and 3a^2-b^2, its underlying rational prime is in {2,17};
- if it divides both R and a^2+b^2, its underlying rational prime is in {2,17}.

Since the active branch has a odd, b even, 17 does not divide a, and the known forcing primes are all odd and different from 17 and 19, every known forcing prime dividing b is automatically coprime to R. This recovers U25's valuation statement by a direct specialization argument and shows that the only new exceptional rational prime introduced by the b-versus-R gcd is 19.

## 3. R versus its conjugate

Let rbar(t) be obtained from r(t) by sqrt(17) -> -sqrt(17). Exact Wolfram computation gives

    Res_t(r,rbar)
      = -6261835300864 sqrt(17)
      = -2^19 * 11 * 13 * 17^4 * sqrt(17).

Hence, after the usual homogenization check using gcd(a,b)=1, any common prime-ideal divisor of R(a,b) and its Galois conjugate is supported above

    {2,11,13,17}.

This is a substantial restriction: away from these four rational primes, the principal ideals generated by R and Rbar are coprime.

## 4. Interaction with the cyclotomic factorization

Over L=K(zeta_5),

    x^5-y^5 = product_{i=0}^4 (x-zeta_5^i y).

Because (x,y)=1, two distinct linear factors can have a common prime ideal only above 5: subtracting two factors gives

    (zeta_5^j-zeta_5^i)y,

and every prime divisor of zeta_5^j-zeta_5^i lies above 5.

Thus outside primes above 5 the five linear factors are pairwise coprime. For any rational prime q dividing b with q not in {2,5,17,19}, U25 and the present R-coprimality calculation imply that the full valuation

    2 v_q(b)=10 v_q(B)

on the right side comes entirely from b^2 and is carried by exactly one of the five pairwise-coprime linear factors above each prime of L over q.

This produces a precise prime-assignment problem in L: every such q contributes a valuation divisible by 10 to exactly one linear factor x-zeta_5^i y.

## 5. What this establishes and what it does not

The factor R is now separated from the large forced divisor structure except at the finite exceptional set

    {2,5,11,13,17,19}.

This is the right shape for a Thue-Mahler or S-unit reduction, but it is not yet one. A proof still needs to control the ideal classes/units in L and convert the pairwise-coprime linear-factor assignments into finitely many explicit equations.
