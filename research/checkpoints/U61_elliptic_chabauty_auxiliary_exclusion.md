## 1. Exact scope and conclusion

The original project target remains the positive-integer equation

    A0^x + B0^y = C0^z,  x,y,z >= 3,

with the assertion `gcd(A0,B0,C0)>1`. The letters below are auxiliary quantities, not those original bases.

**Theorem A (the U60 system).** There are no integers r,s,m,n,A,B satisfying all of

    gcd(r,s)=1,  r and s of opposite parity,
    m+i n=(r+i s)^17,
    gcd(m,n)=gcd(A,B)=1,  mn != 0,
    m^4+30m^2n^2+n^4-16m^2n^2*omega=(A+B*omega)^17,
    omega=(1+sqrt(5))/2.

A Gaussian unit causes no additional case: its seventeenth power is itself and can be absorbed in r+i s.

This is an exclusion, not just a new congruence condition. It finishes the **explicit auxiliary system** pursued in U60. It does not assert that every Beal counterexample, every all-even signature, or every coefficient-2 equation reaches this system. The upstream source/coverage chain remains a separate audit obligation.

The core new result is independent of that upstream chain:

**Theorem B (positive region of a genus-three curve).** Put

    g(u)=u^8+u^7-7u^6-6u^5+15u^4+10u^3-10u^2-4u+1.

There is no rational solution of `g(u)=17*w^2` with `u>2`.

We prove B by an explicit quadratic-field elliptic descent, then deduce A using U60. We do NOT claim to classify the other real regions of the genus-three curve.

## 2. From U60 to the compact curve

For an assumed solution of A, U60 proves that positive integers b,c exist with

    B=-17*b^2,  P16(A,B)=17*c^2,

where `B*P16(A,B)` is the omega coefficient of `(A+B*omega)^17`. In particular B is nonzero. This implication was not inferred from finite search: U60 includes its modulo-289 polynomial Bezout certificate and valuation argument.

Both real embeddings of the left-hand algebraic integer in A are positive: its coefficients in the sqrt(5) basis are `m^4+(22 +/- 8*sqrt(5))*m^2*n^2+n^4`, and `22-8*sqrt(5)>0`. Because 17 is odd, both embeddings of A+B*omega are positive. Consequently

    v=A^2+A*B-B^2 > 0.

Let `T0=2*A+B`, so `T0^2-5*B^2=4*v`. The exact coefficient identity used in U60 is

    2^16*P16(A,B) = sum(j=0..8, binomial(17,2j+1)*5^j*T0^(16-2j)*B^(2j)).

Define

    x=(T0/B)^2,
    f8(x)=sum(j=0..8, binomial(17,2j+1)*5^j*x^(8-j)).

Then `f8(x)=17*(256*c/B^8)^2` and x>5. The exact substitution

    u=2*(x+5)/(x-5)=2+5*B^2/v,
    w=c/v^4

gives `g(u)=17*w^2` and **u>2**. Its polynomial certificate is

    (u-2)^8*f8(5*(u+2)/(u-2)) = 2^16*5^8*g(u).

All denominators here are nonzero under the stated hypotheses. Thus Theorem B contradicts an assumed solution of A. The axis case mn=0 is explicitly outside A: it was already separated in U60 and is not eliminated by division.

## 3. A single quartic twist over Q(sqrt(17))

This section starts only with a rational point `g(u)=17*w^2`, u>2.

Write `pi=sqrt(17)`, `epsilon=4+pi`, and `K=Q(pi)`. This is a DIFFERENT quadratic field from U60's Q(sqrt(5)). Let

    gplus(u)=u^4+(1+pi)/2*u^3+(-3+pi)/2*u^2+(2-pi)*u-1,
    gminus(u)=the conjugate of gplus(u).

Exact algebra gives

    g=gplus*gminus,
    Res(gplus,gminus)=-17^2,
    gplus(2)=17+4*pi=pi*epsilon,
    disc(g)=17^7,
    z^8*g(z+1/z)=1+z+...+z^16.

The final identity identifies every root of g as `2*cos(2*j*pi/17)`, j=1,...,8, with the usual circular constant in this cosine expression. In particular all roots of both monic quartic factors lie strictly between -2 and 2. Thus both conjugates of gplus(u) are positive for u>2.

### 3.1 Class group and units — explicit calculations

The ring of integers is `O_K=Z[(1+pi)/2]`, with discriminant 17. The real-quadratic Minkowski bound is sqrt(17)/2<3. Hence the class group is generated by prime ideals of norm 2. The two primes above 2 are principal, generated by `(5+pi)/2` and `(5-pi)/2`, each of norm 2. Therefore the class number is 1. The splitting also follows from `w0^2-w0-4` modulo 2, where `w0=(1+pi)/2`.

The unit group is `+/- epsilon^Z`. Here is a check of the fundamental unit rather than an unsupported table lookup. The norm of epsilon is -1. Dirichlet's unit theorem gives rank one. If a positive unit eta=(a+b*pi)/2 satisfied 1<eta<epsilon, then

    b=(eta-eta')/pi,
    eta'=+/- eta^(-1).

In either norm case, 0<b<2: the upper bounds are `(epsilon+epsilon^(-1))/pi=2` and `(epsilon-epsilon^(-1))/pi=8/pi<2`. Hence b=1, but the norm equation would require a^2=13 or 21, impossible. Thus no smaller positive generator is missing. Modulo squares the unit representatives are `1,-1,epsilon,-epsilon`.

The Minkowski and Dirichlet statements used here are standard unconditional theorems; precise inspected sources are recorded in section 10. The calculations specific to K are supplied above.

### 3.2 Ideal separation and the unique positive twist

Put u=a/d in lowest terms, d>0, and homogenize the two quartics to Gplus(a,d) and Gminus(a,d). Since g has degree eight,

    Gplus(a,d)*Gminus(a,d)=17*e^2,  e=d^4*w in Z.

To justify e being integral, a rational number e with 17*e^2 integral has denominator whose square divides 17, hence denominator 1.

We have `g(u)=(u-2)^8 (mod17)`. Consequently 17 does not divide d (otherwise the left side of the homogeneous equation is a^8 modulo 17), and

    a=2*d (mod17).

If a prime ideal divides d, neither quartic value is divisible by it, because each reduces to a^4. At every other prime ideal not above 17 the resultant makes the two values coprime. Their product is 17 times a square, so each valuation there is even.

There is only one prime above 17, generated by pi. At this prime,

    Gplus(a,d)=Gplus(2*d,d) (mod pi^2)
               =pi*epsilon*d^4 (mod pi^2).

Its valuation is exactly 1; the same is true of its conjugate. Since the class number is 1, ideal factorization therefore gives

    Gplus(a,d)=unit*pi*beta^2,  beta in O_K.

The signs in the two real embeddings now determine the unit square class. The signs of pi and epsilon are both (+,-); only `pi*epsilon` among `+/- pi` and `+/- pi*epsilon` is totally positive. Absorbing a square unit into beta gives

    gplus(u)=(17+4*pi)*z^2,  z=beta/d^2 in K.       (Q)

Thus every rational point with u>2 lifts to this **one** quartic twist. Neither unique factorization of elements nor the unit sign has been assumed without justification.

## 4. Explicit elliptic model and inverse map

Set t=u-2 and define

    a4=1-4*pi/17,
    b4=(13-3*pi)/2,
    c4=(23-5*pi)/2,
    d4=6-pi.

Equation (Q) becomes

    z^2=1+d4*t+c4*t^2+b4*t^3+a4*t^4.             (Q0)

For t nonzero set

    X=(2*(z+1)+d4*t)/t^2,
    Y=((X^2-4*a4)*t-d4*X-2*b4)/2,
    x=X-(pi-5)/2,  y=Y.

Direct substitution gives the elliptic curve

    E: y^2=x^3+aE*x^2+bE*x,
    aE=4-pi=-epsilon^(-1),
    bE=-8+33*pi/17=epsilon^(-2)/pi.              (E)

The inverse coordinate is the rational function

    U(x,y)=2 + (2*y+d4*X+2*b4)/(X^2-4*a4),
    X=x+(pi-5)/2.                               (U)

Recover t=U-2 and `z=(X*t^2-d4*t-2)/2`. The two t=0 points correspond to

    O = the identity of E,    U(O)=2,
    P = (17/4-pi, -16+31*pi/8),    U(P)=2.

The forward map at (t,z)=(0,1) extends to O; the other point gives P. Because `Norm(a4)=1/17` is not a rational square, a4 is not a square in K. Hence Q0 has no K-rational points at infinity, and the inverse denominator `X^2-4*a4` never vanishes at a finite K-point. This accounts for the exceptional points instead of discarding them.

The curve is nonsingular: `aE^2-4*bE=epsilon^(-3)/pi` and `16*bE^2*(aE^2-4*bE)` is nonzero.

We next prove the stronger elliptic statement

    { Q in E(K) : U(Q) in Q } = {O,P}.           (C)

Equation (C), together with section 3, proves Theorem B.

## 5. Rank one by a complete 2-isogeny descent

### 5.1 The Kummer facts used, and why they apply over K

For a field of characteristic zero and a nonsingular curve

    E(a,b): y^2=x^3+a*x^2+b*x,

let `E'=E(-2*a,a^2-4*b)`. The isogeny and its dual have x-coordinates

    phi_x=x+a+b/x,
    psi_x=(x'-2*a+(a^2-4*b)/x')/4,

and y-coordinates `phi_y=y*(1-b/x^2)` and
`psi_y=y'*(1-(a^2-4*b)/x'^2)/8`. They extend over O and the two-torsion points; their composition is [2]. The formulas can be checked directly in the curve coordinate rings.

The map alpha from E(a,b)(K) to K*/K*^2 sends O to 1, (0,0) to b, and an ordinary point to its x-coordinate. It is a homomorphism: the product of the three nonzero x-coordinates cut out by a line is the square of its intercept. Tangencies follow by the same repeated-root equation, and translation by (0,0) replaces x by b/x. The kernel is psi(E'(K)). Indeed psi_x is a square. Conversely, when x=s^2 and y is nonzero, a preimage has

    x'=a+2*s^2+2*y/s,
    y'=2*s*x'.

Substitution checks the preimage; the zero/torsion cases give the specified value b. This also proves the corresponding assertion for alpha' on E'. No theorem restricted to rational rather than number-field points has been silently applied here.

We use Mordell-Weil finite generation to turn these group indices into a rank.

### 5.2 Integral coefficients and a finite square-class list

Use the isomorphic integral model `bar_x=17*x`, `bar_y=17*pi*y`. Its coefficients and its partner's last coefficient are

    a=17*(4-pi),
    b=17*pi*epsilon^(-2),
    b'=a^2-4*b=17*pi*epsilon^(-3).

All prime-ideal support of b and b' is at pi. For a prime ideal not dividing b, an x-coordinate has even valuation: if negative, the x^3 term uniquely has lowest valuation; if positive, the b*x term does. In both cases comparison with y^2 proves evenness. The same applies to E'. Class number one and the unit calculation reduce both Kummer images to the eight representatives

    1, pi, epsilon, epsilon*pi, -1, -pi, -epsilon, -epsilon*pi.

At the positive real embedding of E, aE<0 and bE>0. If x<0, then `x^2+aE*x+bE>0`, making y^2 negative. Thus alpha(E(K)) contains at most the four positive representatives. It contains pi from T=(0,0), and epsilon*pi from P because

    x(P)=pi/(4*epsilon)=epsilon*pi*(1/(2*epsilon))^2.

These two classes are independent: pi has odd pi-valuation, while epsilon is a nonsquare unit of norm -1. Thus

    |alpha(E(K))|=4.                              (5.1)

### 5.3 Two explicit local obstructions bound the partner image

For a candidate class gamma on the integral partner E', local solubility requires

    W^2=gamma*V0^4+(-2*a)*V0^2*V1^2+(b'/gamma)*V1^4,

with V0,V1 not both divisible by the local uniformizer. The identity follows by substituting `x'=gamma*(V0/V1)^2`; axis cases are included.

The rational prime 2 splits in K. In the first embedding choose `pi=7 (mod16)`, and in the second choose `pi=41 (mod64)`. These are actual 2-adic embeddings: the corresponding values of `(1+pi)/2` are roots 4 modulo 8 and 21 modulo 32 of `w0^2-w0-4`, with odd derivative, so Hensel lifting applies.

The following exhaustive **finite** tests retain all primitive pairs, including zeros:

| Place / modulus | -2a | b' | Square classes not excluded |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| pi=7 mod16 | 6 | 13 | 1, epsilon*pi, -pi, -epsilon |
| pi=41 mod64 | 42 | 53 | 1, pi, epsilon, epsilon*pi |

The domains are respectively 192 and 3072 primitive ordered pairs per class. Independent Python, TypeScript bigint, and Wolfram enumerations give these same lists, with full counts in the output files. This is only an upper bound on local solubility, which is all the proof needs.

Their intersection is `{1,epsilon*pi}`. Both classes actually occur globally, from O' and T', because b' is epsilon*pi times a square. Therefore

    |alpha'(E'(K))|=2.                            (5.2)

### 5.4 Converting the indices to the exact rank

The class of T' is nontrivial in `E'(K)/phi(E(K))`, since b' is not a square. By (5.2), T' generates that quotient. Therefore

    E'(K)=phi(E(K))+ker(psi),
    psi(E'(K))=2E(K).

By (5.1), `|E(K)/2E(K)|=4`. The curve has precisely two K-rational points of order dividing 2: the other two would require a square root of `aE^2-4*bE`, whose norm is 1/17 and hence not a rational square. For a finitely generated group this gives

    2^(rank+1)=4,  so rank E(K)=1.

This is an unconditional algebraic rank argument, not a numerical analytic-rank estimate or a Magma/Sage assertion.

## 6. Torsion, finite fields, and a 3-saturation certificate

### 6.1 Exact finite-field checks

The curve has good reduction above 3,5,7: the displayed discriminant is a unit there. Each of these primes is inert in K. Exact enumeration gives

| Residue field | #E | order of the reduction of P |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| F9 | 8 | 8 |
| F25 | 22 | 22 |
| F49 | 60 | 20 |

TypeScript uses pairs a+b*pi with pi^2=17; Wolfram independently enumerates the same quadratic algebras. Reduction injects the prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion. Comparing 8 and 22, including using the other prime to exclude the 3- and 5-primary parts, shows that E(K)_tors has order dividing 2. Since T exists,

    E(K)_tors={O,T}.

In particular P is not torsion. The F49 row is a corroborating check, not needed for the main proof.

### 6.2 We do not assume that P generates E(K)

Write `E(K)=Z*R + {O,T}` for some unknown generator R, and `P=k*R+e*T` with nonzero integer k. We need only show 3 does not divide k, not determine k.

Let M=8P, and let `tau=-x/y` be the local parameter at O. Exact rational arithmetic gives

    tau(M)=6+6*pi (mod9),
    tau(M)/3=2+2*pi (mod3).                       (6.1)

Full coordinates of M and the full two rational coefficients of tau(M) are in `U61_elliptic_output.json`; the latter were independently recomputed in Wolfram and agree exactly. Their common denominator is a 3-adic unit.

If 3 divided k, then `8P=k*(8R)`, and 8R is in the first formal subgroup because #E(F9)=8. In an integral formal group, multiplication by 3 sends a parameter in 3*O to 9*O: the linear term is 3*tau and every higher term has degree at least two. This would put tau(M) in 9*O, contradicting (6.1). Hence

    3 does not divide k.                          (6.2)

This supplies the precise saturation needed below without an unproved assertion that P is a full Mordell-Weil generator.

## 7. Elliptic Chabauty at 3 — exclusion of every other rational coordinate

This section contains the global step that finite residue tests alone could not provide.

Let `L=K at its prime over 3=Q3(pi)` and `O_L=Z3[pi]`. This is the unramified quadratic extension, since `pi^2=17` reduces to an irreducible quadratic over F3.

### 7.1 Only two residue disks are possible

The inverse denominator in (U) has no zero on any finite point over F9. Indeed `Norm(a4)=1/17=2 (mod3)` is a nonsquare in F3, so a4 cannot be a square in F9. At O, (U) extends regularly with value 2. Thus U is integral and regular throughout every L-rational residue disk of E.

An exhaustive evaluation on the **eight** points of E(F9) shows that the points with U in F3 are exactly

    Obar,
    Pbar=(2+2*pi, 2+2*pi),

and both have U=2. TypeScript and Wolfram independently give this same two-point list. Any K-rational point with U in Q must therefore lie in the disk of O or of P.

### 7.2 Every global point in either disk lies on one formal line

Use the invariant differential `omega_E=dx/(2*y)` and its formal logarithm, normalized by `log_E(tau)=tau+...`. It is an injective analytic homomorphism from E1(L) to 3*O_L, with an inverse on 3*O_L. To see the relevant convergence directly, integrate the invariant differential in tau: its coefficients lie in O_L, so the coefficient of tau^n in the logarithm lies in (1/n)*O_L. For tau in 3*O_L every term after tau has valuation at least two, and the logarithm has unit derivative. The nonlinear part is a strict contraction on 3*O_L, giving an inverse there; invariance of the differential gives additivity.

Set `lambda=log_E(M)`. From (6.1),

    lambda/3=2+2*pi (mod3),  v3(lambda)=1.         (7.1)

Let Q be a global point in the disk of S, where S=O or P, and put Q0=Q-S in E1(L). Express Q0 as `n*R+f*T`. Since `8P=k*(8R)`, additivity gives

    log_E(Q0)= n/(8*k) * lambda.

By (6.2), `z0=n/(8*k)` lies in Z3. Thus

    Q=S+exp_E(z0*lambda),  z0 in Z3.              (7.2)

This is a containment of all **global** points in an analytic line, not an assertion that every point of a two-dimensional local elliptic disk is generated by M.

### 7.3 The rationality equation has one zero on each line

Expand the function

    F_S(z)=U(S+exp_E(z*lambda)).

It has coefficients in L and constant term U(S)=2. Let `D` be the invariant derivation dual to omega_E. Because U is integral and regular on the disk and D preserves its integral formal power-series ring,

    F_S(z)=U(S)+sum(n>=1, D^n U(S)*lambda^n*z^n/n!),
    D^n U(S) in O_L.

For n>=2,

    v3(lambda^n/n!) >= n-v3(n!) >= 2,

and this lower bound tends to infinity. Thus every nonlinear coefficient is in 9*O_L, and the series converges for every z in Z3.

The needed first derivatives are exact:

    D U(O)=-2,  D U(P)=2.

At O, the first is seen from `x=tau^(-2)-aE+O(tau^2)`, `y=-x/tau`, which gives `U=2-2*tau+O(tau^2)`. At P it follows either from the quartic involution or from the directly checked formula

    D U = 2*y*(partial U/partial x)
          +(3*x^2+2*aE*x+bE)*(partial U/partial y).

The latter was evaluated exactly in both SymPy and Wolfram.

Take the coefficient of pi in F_S(z), using the integral basis 1,pi at 3. Its constant term is zero. By (7.1), its linear coefficient divided by 3 is respectively `-4=2 (mod3)` or `4=1 (mod3)`, a unit. All nonlinear coefficients divided by 3 are divisible by 3. Therefore

    coeff_pi(F_S(z))=3*z*(unit+3*h_S(z)),

where h_S is a convergent Z3-valued power series on Z3. The parenthesized expression is a unit at every z in Z3. Consequently the pi coefficient vanishes **only at z=0**.

If U(Q) is rational, that coefficient must vanish. Equations (7.2) and injectivity of the logarithm now force Q=S. This proves (C): the only K-points with rational U are O and P, both with U=2.

It follows that no rational u>2 can satisfy g(u)=17*w^2, proving B. Section 2 then proves A.

This proof bounds infinitely many points through a formal-series unit argument. It does not turn a finite enumeration into an infinite nonexistence assertion.

## 9. Other investigations and failed shortcuts retained

The genus-three Jacobian is not rank zero. Counts of its curve over F_p, F_(p^2), F_(p^3), for p=3,5,7,11,13, give Jacobian orders 22,56,206,1178,2768. Their gcd is 2. Thus rational torsion is at most order two. The divisor `(2,17)-(2,-17)` on the equivalent model `y^2=17*g(u)` is neither zero nor order two: either would create a degree-one function, or a degree-two function ramified at a non-Weierstrass point, contradicting genus three and uniqueness of its hyperelliptic degree-two map. This rules out the early rank-zero shortcut. These genus-three counts are not used in the main elliptic proof.

An exploratory exact scan of nP+eT for -6<=n<=6 and e=0,1 found rational U only for O and P. The scan is preserved as a bounded diagnostic and is NOT the completeness argument.

At 5, only two residue disks also survive, but tau(22P)/5 is rational modulo 5, so the simple first-order rationality test there is degenerate. Switching to 3, rather than increasing a blind search, supplied the unit linear coefficient used above.

An early SymPy audit used syntactic equality before fully normalizing algebraic coefficients and falsely flagged several identities; the checker was repaired to use polynomials over Q(sqrt(17)) and all checks passed. One Wolfram norm substitution similarly failed to conjugate inverse square roots; explicit conjugate multiplication returned the corrected norms 1/17 and -1/17. Neither faulty intermediate output is used as evidence.
