## 2. Galois Closure $L$ of $F$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

### 2.1 Subfields and Polynomial Factorization
The base field is the real quadratic field $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}) = \mathbb{Q}(\phi)$, where $\phi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.
The roots of the quadratic equation $Y^2 + 2Y - 4 = 0$ are:

$$\bar{\alpha} = -1 - \sqrt{5} = -2\phi < 0.$$
Over $k$, the degree-34 polynomial $f(T) = T^{34} + 2T^{17} - 4$ factors as:
$$f(T) = (T^{17} - \alpha)(T^{17} - \bar{\alpha}).$$

The extension $F/k = k(s)$ is generated by a root $s = \alpha^{1/17}$.
Because $(2)$ is inert in $k$ ($\mathcal{O}_k/(2) \cong \mathbb{F}_4$) and $v_{(2)}(\alpha) = v_{(2)}(2/\phi) = 1$, the polynomial $T^{17} - \alpha$ is Eisenstein at the prime ideal $(2)$ in $\mathcal{O}_k$. Thus, $[F:k] = 17$, $[F:\mathbb{Q}] = 34$, and $f(T)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

The 34 roots of $f(T)$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are:
$$s_{1, j} = \zeta_{17}^j \alpha^{1/17}, \quad s_{2, m} = \zeta_{17}^m \bar{\alpha}^{1/17}, \quad (0 \le j, m \le 16),$$
where $\zeta_{17} = e^{2\pi i / 17}$.

To split $f(T)$, any splitting field $L$ must contain:

   Since $\gcd(\operatorname{cond}(k), \operatorname{cond}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{17}))) = \gcd(5, 17) = 1$, these fields are linearly disjoint:
   $$K_1 = k(\zeta_{17}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}, \zeta_{17}), \quad [K_1:\mathbb{Q}] = 2 \times 16 = 32.$$
3. The Kummer extensions adjoining $\alpha^{1/17}$ and $\bar{\alpha}^{1/17}$ to $K_1$:

This proves unconditionally that $\alpha$ and $\bar{\alpha}$ generate independent Kummer extensions of degree 17 over $K_1$.
Therefore:
$$[L : K_1] = 17 \times 17 = 289 = 17^2.$$

$$[L : \mathbb{Q}] = [L : K_1] \cdot [K_1 : \mathbb{Q}] = 17^2 \cdot 32 = 9248 = 34 \times 17 \times 16.$$

## 3. The Relative Norm Map and 2-Class Group Splitting

$$N_{F/k}: \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F) \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k), \quad [\mathfrak{A}] \mapsto [N_{F/k}(\mathfrak{A})].$$

For any ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subset \mathcal{O}_k$:
$$(N_{F/k} \circ j)([\mathfrak{a}]) = [\mathfrak{a}^{[F:k]}] = [\mathfrak{a}^{17}] = 17 [\mathfrak{a}].$$

Let $A[2^\infty]$ denote the 2-primary component (2-Sylow subgroup) of a finite abelian group $A$.

$$[17] \in \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)[2^\infty]).$$

This yields the following algebraic consequences:
1. $j: \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)[2^\infty] \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2^\infty]$ is strictly injective.
2. $N_{F/k}: \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2^\infty] \to \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)[2^\infty]$ is surjective.
3. The 2-primary class group decomposes as an internal direct sum:
   $$\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2^\infty] \cong j(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)[2^\infty]) \oplus \ker(N_{F/k})[2^\infty].$$

Now, for $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$:
The discriminant is $\operatorname{disc}(k) = 5$.

Consequently, $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)[2^\infty] = 0$, which yields the exact equality:
$$\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2^\infty] = \ker(N_{F/k})[2^\infty].$$

## 4. Galois Representation Rigidity and Cohen-Martinet Heuristics

### 4.1 Representation-Theoretic Rigidity of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2]$

The polynomial algebra is:
$$\mathbb{F}_2[P] \cong \mathbb{F}_2[X] / (X^{17} - 1) = \mathbb{F}_2[X] / ((X - 1) \Phi_{17}(X)).$$

What is the factorization of the 17th cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{17}(X)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$?
The multiplicative order of 2 modulo 17 is:
$$2^1 = 2, \; 2^2 = 4, \; 2^3 = 8, \; 2^4 \equiv 16 \equiv -1, \; 2^8 \equiv 1 \pmod{17} \implies \operatorname{ord}_{17}(2) = 8.$$
Therefore, over $\mathbb{F}_2$, $\Phi_{17}(X)$ factors into $(17-1)/8 = 2$ irreducible polynomials of degree 8:
$$\Phi_{17}(X) = (X^8 + X^5 + X^4 + X^3 + 1)(X^8 + X^7 + X^6 + X^4 + X^2 + X + 1) \pmod 2.$$

**Theorem (Galois Rigidity of 2-Torsion):**
Every non-trivial irreducible $\mathbb{F}_2[P]$-module has dimension **8**.
Since $h(k) = 1$, the $P$-fixed submodule $(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_N)[2])^P$ (which corresponds via class field theory and the genus formula to unramified abelian 2-extensions of $k$) is trivial.
Therefore, if $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2]$ were non-trivial, it could NOT have $\mathbb{F}_2$-dimension $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6,$ or $7$.
$$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2} \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_F)[2] \in \{0\} \cup \{d \in \mathbb{Z} : d \ge 8\}.$$
