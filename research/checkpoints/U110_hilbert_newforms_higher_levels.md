## 3. Rigorous Proof: Non-Existence of Norm 4000 Ideals

**Theorem 1.** *There does not exist any integral ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$ such that $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a}) = 4000$.*

*Proof.*
1. Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$ be any non-zero ideal. By the unique factorization of ideals in Dedekind domains:
   $$\mathfrak{a} = \prod_{\mathfrak{q}} \mathfrak{q}^{c_{\mathfrak{q}}}, \quad c_{\mathfrak{q}} \ge 0.$$
2. The norm map is completely multiplicative:
   $$\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a}) = \prod_{\mathfrak{q}} \operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q})^{c_{\mathfrak{q}}}.$$
3. Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a}) = 4000$.
4. The rational integer 4000 has prime factorization:
   $$4000 = 2^5 \cdot 5^3.$$
5. If $\mathfrak{q}$ is a prime ideal dividing $\mathfrak{a}$, its norm $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}) = q^{f(\mathfrak{q}/q)}$ must divide 4000. Therefore, the underlying rational prime $q = \mathfrak{q} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ must divide 4000.
6. Thus, $q \in \{2, 5\}$, which implies that the only prime ideals dividing $\mathfrak{a}$ are $\mathfrak{p}_2$ and $\mathfrak{p}_5$.
7. Hence, $\mathfrak{a}$ must be uniquely of the form:
   $$\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{p}_2^u \mathfrak{p}_5^v \quad \text{for integers } u, v \ge 0.$$
8. Evaluating the norm of $\mathfrak{a}$:
   $$\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a}) = \operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p}_2)^u \cdot \operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p}_5)^v = 4^u \cdot 5^v = (2^2)^u \cdot 5^v = 2^{2u} \cdot 5^v.$$
9. Setting this equal to 4000:
   $$2^{2u} \cdot 5^v = 2^5 \cdot 5^3.$$
10. By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic in $\mathbb{Z}$, the exponents of each prime must match:
    $$2u = 5 \quad \text{and} \quad v = 3.$$
11. Since $u \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, $2u$ is an even integer. However, 5 is an odd integer.
12. The equation $2u = 5$ has no integer solution ($u = 5/2 \notin \mathbb{Z}$).
13. Therefore, no such ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ exists in $\mathcal{O}_K$. $\blacksquare$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Source of the "Norm 4000" Misconception**:
> In classical modular forms over $\mathbb{Q}$, multiplying a level $N = 2000$ by 2 produces level $2N = 4000$. However, over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, multiplying an ideal by 2 multiplies its norm by $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(2) = 4$. Thus, passing from level $\mathfrak{N}_{2000}$ to $2 \mathfrak{N}_{2000}$ produces level $(40\sqrt{5})$ of norm $2000 \times 4 = \mathbf{8000}$, completely skipping 4000. Any reference to a "norm 4000 Hilbert newform" over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ is mathematically vacuous.

## 6. Serre and Breuil--Diamond Conductor Lowering Rigidity

We now prove that **no modular form at any sub-level or higher level can match the Frey representation $\bar\rho_{J^-, 17}$**.

### 6.1 Local Artin Conductor Exponent at $\mathfrak{p}_2$
Let $J^- = \operatorname{Jac}(C^-(U, V))$ be the Frey Jacobian for a primitive solution of $X^{10} + 256Y^{10} = Z^{17}$ with $Y > 0$.
1. Over the local completion $K_{\mathfrak{p}_2} \cong \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{5})$ (which is unramified of degree 2 over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, $e=1, f=2$), the hyperelliptic curve $C^-(U, V)$ acquires good reduction over $L = K_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(2^{1/5})$.
2. The local Galois group is the dihedral group $D_5$, and the inertia group is cyclic of order 5:
   $$I_{\mathfrak{p}_2} \cong C_5, \quad |I_{\mathfrak{p}_2}| = 5.$$
3. Since $p = 2 \ne 5$, this ramification is **strictly tame**. The higher ramification subgroups vanish: $I_1 = \{1\}$.
4. The 2-dimensional RM factor $V$ has no inertia invariants: $V^{I_{\mathfrak{p}_2}} = \{0\}$.
5. By the Artin conductor formula:
   $$n(\mathfrak{p}_2) = \dim V - \dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{p}_2}} = 2 - 0 = \mathbf{2}.$$
6. **Lowering Invariance**: Because $\gcd(|I_{\mathfrak{p}_2}|, 17) = \gcd(5, 17) = 1$, the tame inertia character of order 5 remains of order 5 modulo 17. The representation $\bar\rho_{J^-, 17}|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_2}}$ cannot become trivial, nor can its invariant space increase.
7. Therefore, the conductor exponent at $\mathfrak{p}_2$ is **strictly 2**:
   $$v_{\mathfrak{p}_2}(\mathfrak{N}) = 2.$$
   Every level with $u = 0$ or $u = 1$ is **strictly ruled out**.

### 6.2 Local Artin Conductor Exponent at $\mathfrak{p}_5$
1. Over $K_{\mathfrak{p}_5} \cong \mathbb{Q}_5(\sqrt{5})$ (which is totally ramified of degree 2 over $\mathbb{Q}_5$, $e=2, f=1$), by U64 Lemma 1 (Uniform Eisenstein Lemma), the polynomial $f(T - U - 2V)$ is Eisenstein at 5 for all primitive solutions with $5 \nmid Z$.
2. The inertia group is the dihedral group of order 10:
   $$I_{\mathfrak{p}_5} \cong D_5, \quad |I_{\mathfrak{p}_5}| = 10, \quad I_1 \cong C_5, \quad I_{\ge 2} = \{1\}.$$
3. The wild inertia group $I_1$ has order 5. Both $V^{I_{\mathfrak{p}_5}} = \{0\}$ and $V^{I_1} = \{0\}$.
4. By the Artin conductor formula with wild Swan component:
   $$n(\mathfrak{p}_5) = (\dim V - \dim V^{I_{\mathfrak{p}_5}}) + \frac{|I_1|}{|I_{\mathfrak{p}_5}|}(\dim V - \dim V^{I_1}) = (2 - 0) + \frac{5}{10}(2 - 0) = 2 + 1 = \mathbf{3}.$$
5. **Lowering Invariance**: Because $\gcd(|I_{\mathfrak{p}_5}|, 17) = \gcd(10, 17) = 1$, the ramification cannot be killed modulo 17.
6. Therefore, the conductor exponent at $\mathfrak{p}_5$ is **strictly 3**:
   $$v_{\mathfrak{p}_5}(\mathfrak{N}) = 3.$$
   Every level with $v \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ is **strictly ruled out**.
