# U72: a root-closed order, full coefficient support, and exact divisibility profiles

## 1. Exact result and dependencies

The active auxiliary problem is

    (W) X^10+256Y^10=Z^17,
        X,Y,Z positive integers, gcd(X,Y)=1.

Write rho for the positive fifth root of 2, set L=Q(rho,i), and put

    c=i*rho^4,  c^10=-256.

U71 supplies a unique integral element theta in L with

    theta^17=X+cY,   Norm(theta)=Z,   289|Y.          (1.1)

The following theorem refines that necessary condition. Its new assertions about coefficients apply to any integral theta satisfying (1.1), with X odd and gcd(X,Y)=1. The application to W inherits U71's written/self-reviewed theorem. It does not invoke Q65, U41, a boundary-curve identification, a modular-form list, or a conjectural rank.

**Theorem.** The element theta lies in the smaller order R=Z[c], whose index in O_L is 2^34. Thus

    theta=a0+a1*c+...+a9*c^9,  a_j in Z.             (1.2)

Let e=v_17(Y), so e>=2. All ten coefficients are nonzero, and

    v_17(a_j)=j(e-1),  j=0,...,9.                    (1.3)

More precisely, with y=Y/17^e,

    a_j/17^(j(e-1)) = X^(1-j)*y^j/j! modulo 17.      (1.4)

Negative exponents in (1.4) mean inverses of the unit X modulo 17.

For every prime q in

    Pset={11,31,41,61,71,101,151,191,211,281,331,431,461,631},

one has q|Y, and the exact additional profile is

    v_q(a_j)=j*v_q(Y)+epsilon(q,j),                  (1.5)

where epsilon is 1 if q=11 and j>=3, or if q=101 and j>=7, and is zero otherwise.

**Weighted corollary.** Put

    P=product(Pset)=433992707035920672833426144791,
    D=11*101*(17P)^9.

Then D divides the nonzero integer a9. Bounds from all ten complex embeddings give

    max(X,Y)>10^4810.                               (1.6)

At least one of X,Y therefore has at least 4811 decimal digits. This replaces the earlier 515-digit necessary lower bound for this auxiliary equation. It is not a search through that range, not an upper bound, and not a lower bound for every possible Beal counterexample.

The structural result is (1.2)–(1.5): the actual common root has no zero coefficient in this fixed power basis, and its coefficients have different, precisely controlled divisibilities. Simply assuming a two-term or three-term root would miss every putative weighted solution. These are still necessary conditions; an integral root with these properties has not been excluded.

## 2. A monogenic maximal order and the index of R

Define

    u=(1-i)*rho^3/2.

Direct algebra gives

    u^5=-1+i,  u^10+2u^5+2=0,
    i=u^5+1,  rho=u^7+u^2,
    c=-u^8-2u^3,  c^4=8rho,  c^5=16i.              (2.1)

The polynomial h(U)=U^10+2U^5+2 is Eisenstein at 2. Thus u is integral, has degree ten, and generates L. Its polynomial discriminant is

    disc(h)=-160000000000=-2^14*5^10.                (2.2)

Only 2 and 5 could divide the index of Z[u] in O_L. At 2, the Eisenstein polynomial makes u a uniformizer in a totally ramified degree-ten extension. Such a uniformizer generates its local integer ring: expand any local integer in powers of u with scalar residue representatives; the finite Z_2-module Z_2[u] is complete and contains the limit. Hence Z_2[u]=O_L tensor Z_2.

At 5, Q_5(rho) is totally ramified of degree five because (U+2)^5-2 is Eisenstein. Its integer ring is Z_5[rho], by the same complete-uniformizer argument. The two simple roots of I^2+1 modulo 5 lift to Q_5, so the local integer algebra of L is Z_5[rho] times itself. It is generated by rho and i. These both belong to Z[u] by (2.1); therefore Z_5[u] is already maximal. Away from 2 and 5, (2.2) excludes any index. This proves

    O_L=Z[u],  disc(L)=-160000000000.                (2.3)

The local ramification and lifting facts are standard; the cited Milne pages were inspected again [S1]. The generator identities and the specialized maximality argument are given here, not inferred from a numerical discriminant label.

The polynomial of c is C^10+256, since (2.1) recovers both rho and i from c over Q. Its discriminant is -2^82*5^10. Consequently

    [O_L:R]=sqrt(disc(C^10+256)/disc(L))=2^34.        (2.4)

Both verifiers also compute (2.4) directly: form the ten columns of 1,c,...,c^9 in the u power basis and take their exact determinant. This provides an integer-lattice check in addition to the discriminant ratio.

## 3. Why taking this seventeenth root does not leave R

An integral root of an element of a nonmaximal order need not belong to that order. The unit hypothesis at its conductor prime is essential here.

For every n>=1, the ring R/2^n R has 2^(10n) elements. Its reduction modulo 2 is F_2[c]/(c^10), a local ring with residue field F_2. Exactly half its elements are units, so

    |(R/2^n R)^*|=2^(10n-1).

The map z -> z^17 is therefore an automorphism of this finite abelian group. Since X is odd, X+cY is a unit in R/2^n R. Its unique roots at successive n are compatible and define a root in the complete ring R tensor Z_2.

The integer ring O_L tensor Z_2 also has residue field F_2, by total ramification at 2. Its finite unit quotients have 2-power order. In particular it has no nonidentity unit whose seventeenth power is one. Theta is a unit there: its norm Z is odd. The root just constructed and theta must thus be equal.

Hence theta lies in R tensor Z_2. At every odd prime, R is already maximal by (2.4). The rational coefficients of theta in the c power basis are integral at every prime and hence are ordinary integers. This proves (1.2).

The proof does not apply to a root that is a nonunit at 2. Four exact local unit examples are checked modulo 2^12: Python uses the inverse exponent in the full finite unit group; TypeScript uses successive digit lifting. Their complete coefficient vectors agree.

## 4. The local binomial series and its exact leading term

For k>=0 write

    B_k=binomial(1/17,k).

The formal series F(z)=sum B_k*z^k satisfies F(z)^17=1+z. For example, its coefficient recurrence gives (1+z)F'=F/17 and F(0)=1, proving the formal identity by differentiation.

### 4.1 Convergence at 17

For k>=1,

    v_17(B_k)=-k-v_17(k!),

because the numerator factors 1-17r are all 17-adic units. If v_17(z)=e>=2, then

    v_17(B_k*z^k)=k(e-1)-v_17(k!).                 (4.1)

The elementary factorial formula gives v_17(k!)<k/16, so these valuations tend to infinity. The series consequently converges in the complete rank-ten algebra with basis 1,c,...,c^9. Its seventeenth power is 1+cz after replacing z by cz; here c is a unit at 17.

### 4.2 The scalar root of X exists

The residue algebra R/17R is reduced, since C^10+256 has no repeated root in characteristic 17. Its Frobenius map is bijective. From theta^17=X modulo 17 it follows that theta=X modulo 17. Write theta=X+17b in the local algebra. Expanding to precision 289 and using 289|Y gives

    X^17=X modulo 289.

The scalar polynomial

    ((X+17H)^17-X)/289

has integral 17-adic coefficients and has derivative X^16 modulo 17, a unit. A root modulo 17 lifts uniquely, providing a scalar x in Z_17 with

    x^17=X,  x=X modulo 17.

In the subgroup 1+17(R tensor Z_17), taking a seventeenth power is injective. Indeed, if h has coefficient valuation k>=1, then (1+h)^17-1 has valuation exactly k+1: the term 17h has this valuation and every higher term has larger valuation. This also works in the product algebra; no assumption that it is a single field is needed.

Therefore the actual local value of theta is

    theta=x*sum_(k>=0) B_k*(Y/X)^k*c^k.             (4.2)

No arbitrary choice of a complex root branch has been made.

### 4.3 Grouping by powers of c proves full support

Using c^10=-256, the j-th coefficient in (4.2) is

    a_j=x*sum_(m>=0) B_(j+10m)*(Y/X)^(j+10m)*(-256)^m.
                                                               (4.3)

For j=0,...,9, the leading term m=0 has valuation j(e-1), since 17 does not divide j!. Every tail index k=j+10m, m>=1, has strictly greater valuation. Explicitly,

    (k-j)(e-1)-v_17(k!)
      >= k-j-(k-1)/16
      >= (151-j)/16 > 8.

Thus every tail term has valuation at least nine above the leading term. Cancellation of that term is impossible. This proves all ten equalities in (1.3), including v_17(a0)=0, and hence proves that every coefficient is nonzero.

Reducing the leading term after dividing by 17^(j(e-1)) gives (1.4): one uses x=X modulo 17 and

    17^j B_j = 1/j! modulo 17, j<17.

This is an all-precision argument. The checks at e=2,3,4, and a nonscalar-parameter example X=2^17, Y=3*17^2, only corroborate it. Python uses the convergent series with a proved tail bound; TypeScript instead lifts the power equation digit by digit. Their vectors agree at the precision genuinely determined by a seventeenth power: one digit less than its output precision at 17.

## 5. The fourteen forced primes and their coefficient profiles

### 5.1 Rechecked upstream divisors, not a new prime search

The Gaussian descent needed here is the one reconstructed in U71 and U68. For clarity its relevant data are as follows. From W,

    X^5+16iY^5=(a+ib)^17, gcd(a,b)=1, a odd, b even.

Write aG=X^5 and bH=16Y^5, with

    G=sum_(r=0)^8 (-1)^r binomial(17,2r)a^(16-2r)b^(2r),
    H=sum_(r=0)^8 (-1)^r binomial(17,2r+1)a^(16-2r)b^(2r).

Rational prime valuations give three cases:

    I:   a=A^5,        b=16B^5,          G=C^5,    H=D^5;
    II:  a=17^4 A^5,   b=16B^5,          G=17C^5,  H=D^5;
    III: a=A^5,        b=16*17^4 B^5,    G=C^5,    H=17D^5.

The exact modulo-11 test excludes I and II. In III, Y=17BD. At each q in Pset, the complete residue test leaves only (A:B)=(1:0), so q|B and q|Y.

These finite domains were rerun in both new verifiers. Python checks all q+1 projective classes. This normalization is valid because scaling A,B by lambda multiplies a,b by lambda^5 and both cofactors by lambda^80, a fifth power. TypeScript does not use that normalization: it checks every pair of fifth-power images, except the simultaneous zero pair. Individual zero coordinates and all signs are retained. All fourteen results agree. The prime list itself is inherited, not a newly discovered restriction.

### 5.2 A scalar root at each of these primes

Each q in Pset is 1 modulo 5. The factor degrees of R^5-2 over F_q are either five fixed roots or one five-cycle: Frobenius is a translation on the fifth-root labels. After adjoining i, all residue degrees in L divide

    d_q=5 if q=1 modulo 4, and d_q=10 if q=3 modulo 4.

For the fourteen q, exact evaluation of q^d_q modulo 17 gives respectively

    15,8,11,6,8,16,4,16,2,8,13,15,15,4.

None is one. Thus none of the residue-field unit groups has 17-torsion. Higher reduction kernels have q-power order and q!=17. Taking a seventeenth power is an automorphism on every finite local unit quotient. The verifiers additionally check c^(q^d_q)=c modulo q by iterated Frobenius, rather than relying on an unverified factor-degree label.

Since q|Y and X is a unit, theta reduces to the unique scalar seventeenth root of X. That scalar root lifts to x_q in Z_q. The actual theta is the binomial series (4.2) with x_q in place of x.

For q!=17, every B_k is q-adically integral. One elementary justification is that 1/17 is in Z_q, nonnegative ordinary integers are dense there, and the continuous polynomial binomial(z,k) takes integral values at those integers; Z_q is closed. Hence the series converges when q|Y.

### 5.3 Exact valuations, including the two extra factors

For j<=9, direct evaluation gives

    v_q(B_j)=epsilon(q,j),

with epsilon as defined in (1.5). The only numerator factors from Pset are 11, entering at j=3, and 101, entering at j=7. In particular,

    B_9=658767450/17^9,
    658767450=2*3*5^2*11*59*67*101.                (5.1)

For e_q=v_q(Y)>=1, the leading term in (4.3) has valuation j e_q+epsilon(q,j). Every remaining term has index at least j+10, hence valuation at least (j+10)e_q. Because epsilon is at most one, it cannot cancel the leading term. This proves the exact profile (1.5), not merely a lower bound.

Combining j=9 at these primes with (1.3) and e>=2 yields

    D=11*101*(17P)^9 divides a9,  a9!=0.             (5.2)

The ninth binomial coefficient, all local profiles, and the root equations are checked exactly. No assumption is made about the primes outside Pset and 17.

## 6. A global embedding bound for the same coefficient

Let kappa=|c|=2^(4/5), so kappa^5=16 and 1<kappa<2. All ten conjugates c_r are c times the ten roots of unity of order dividing ten. The corresponding actual conjugates theta_r satisfy

    |theta_r|^17=|X+c_rY|<=X+kappa Y.

Put H=(X+kappa Y)^(1/17). Fourier inversion on the ten powers gives

    |a_j| kappa^j <= (1/10) sum_r |theta_r| <= H.

There is no assumption that the conjugates use a common analytic seventeenth-root branch. Applying j=9 and (5.2), with T=max(X,Y), gives

    kappa^9 D <= H,
    T >= kappa^153 D^17/(1+kappa).

Raising to the fifth power and using 1+kappa<3 proves

    T^5 > 16^153 D^85/243.                          (6.1)

Exact integer exponentiation verifies

    16^153 D^85 > 243*10^24050,

so T>10^4810, as stated. The same deliberately coarse bound does not reach 10^4811; no assertion of an optimal threshold is made. Python, TypeScript and Wolfram agree on this integer comparison. No floating-point logarithm, decimal root approximation, or search through possible X,Y is used to decide it.

## 7. Follow-up that survives, and the remaining global problem

The coefficient conditions are compatible locally. At 17, X=1,Y=17^2 gives the convergent series (4.2), an actual local root with all ten coefficients and valuations 0,1,...,9. Its norm gives a local Z satisfying W. The exact finite examples retain this case and the nontrivial scalar example. They are not positive-integer solutions of W.

Thus (1.3) is not an infinite descent to Y=0, and increasing precision cannot by itself eliminate this residue disk. Likewise, replacing c by a large multiple to absorb coefficient divisors changes the chosen order; it does not produce a smaller solution of the same fixed problem.

The new global target can be stated without a guessed sparse root: find or exclude an integer vector (a0,...,a9), all entries nonzero and with the proven profiles, such that in Q[c]/(c^10+256)

    coefficient_j((sum a_r c^r)^17)=0 for j=2,...,9,

while the constant and linear coefficients are positive and coprime, and the norm is as in U71. No classification of the rational or integral points on this system, no upper height bound, and no contradiction for its unbounded exceptional-prime choices has been proved here. A proof addressing that global issue remains the priority; another extension of the same forcing-prime list alone would not resolve it.
