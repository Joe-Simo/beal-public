## 2. Mathematical Foundation of Higher Trace Rigidity

### 2.1 The Kraus Frey Model and Serre Conductor
To any primitive solution $(A, B, C)$ of $A^2 + B^3 = C^{11}$, we associate the Kraus Frey elliptic curve:
$$E_{(A, B)}: Y^2 = X^3 + 3BX + 2A.$$
Its geometric invariants are:
$$c_4 = -144 B, \quad c_6 = -1728 A, \quad \Delta = -2^6 \cdot 3^3 C^{11} = -1728 C^{11}.$$
- For any prime $q \ge 5$ with $q \nmid C$, $E_{(A, B)}$ has **good reduction** at $q$.
- For any prime $q \ge 5$ with $q \mid C$, since $\gcd(A, B) = 1$, $q \nmid B$, hence $v_q(c_4) = 0$ and $v_q(\Delta) = 11 v_q(C) > 0$. Therefore, $E_{(A, B)}$ has **multiplicative reduction** at $q$.
- By Ribet's level-lowering theorem, the mod-11 representation $\bar{\rho}_{E, 11}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_{11})$ is unramified at all $q \ge 5$ with $q \mid C$.
- The Serre conductor divides $N \mid 2^8 \cdot 3^5 = 62208$. After local analysis at 2 and 3 (Dahmen 2008, Freitas--Naskręcki--Stoll 2020), the Serre level divides $2^5 \cdot 3^3 = 864$.

### 2.2 Difference Intervals and Rigidity Ranges
By the Eichler--Shimura relation, for any rational weight-2 newform $f \in S_2^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_0(N))$ associated with $\bar{\rho}_{E, 11}$:
1. If $q \nmid C$ (good reduction):
   $$a_q(E_{(A, B)}) \equiv a_q(f) \pmod{11}.$$
2. If $q \mid C$ (multiplicative reduction):
   $$a_q(f) \equiv \pm(q + 1) \pmod{11}.$$

Both $a_q(E)$ and $a_q(f)$ are rational integers satisfying the Hasse--Weil bound:
$$|a_q| \le M_q := \lfloor 2\sqrt{q} \rfloor.$$
The difference $D = a_q(E) - a_q(f)$ lies in the interval $[-2M_q, 2M_q]$.
Because $D \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$, $D$ must be an integer multiple of 11:
$$D \in 11\mathbb{Z} \cap [-2M_q, 2M_q].$$

**Definition (Rigid Interval)**:
For a good prime $q$, the *exact integer rigidity range* $\mathcal{R}_q \subset [-M_q, M_q]$ is the set of values $t \in [-M_q, M_q]$ such that:
$$\{x \in [-M_q, M_q] : x \equiv t \pmod{11}\} = \{t\}.$$
For any newform $f$ with $a_q(f) \in \mathcal{R}_q$, the congruence $a_q(E) \equiv a_q(f) \pmod{11}$ implies the exact equality **$a_q(E) = a_q(f) \in \mathbb{Z}$**.
